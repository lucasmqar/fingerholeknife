Gifts for men who cook

02An instant-read thermometer

The single cheapest thing that makes steak, chicken and pork come out right. Look for a reading in a few seconds and a probe that folds away.

03A big wooden cutting board

Most home boards are too small, so food ends up on the counter. Something around 18 × 12 inches, in maple, walnut or another hardwood, with rubber feet or a groove for juices.

04A honing rod

A few strokes before cooking keeps an edge cutting longer between sharpenings. Pairs well with a new knife.

05A cast-iron skillet

Lasts decades, sears better than nonstick, goes from stove to oven. Pre-seasoned ones are ready on day one.

06A good apron

Heavy cotton or canvas, adjustable neck strap, a pocket for a towel and a thermometer. Boring, used every night.

07A cookbook for the food he already loves

Barbecue, Italian, Mexican, Korean: match what he already cooks, not what you wish he would.

08A spice kit he wouldn't buy himself

Smoked paprika, whole cumin, a real chili flake, flaky salt. Small jars beat big ones; spices fade.

··Quick answers

What do you get a man who cooks and has everything?

Something he uses daily but wouldn't upgrade himself: an unusual knife (like one with a finger hole), a bigger cutting board, or a fast thermometer.

Is a knife a good gift?

Yes, if it's one he'll actually use for everyday cooking and you keep the receipt. A single good chef knife beats a block of knives he won't touch.
