Buying a knife from an online ad: seven checks

Before you pay for a knife you saw in an ad, check three things: who actually runs the checkout, what the cart total really is, and how you'd get a refund. The rest of this page is how to check each one in about two minutes.

01The seven checks

  1. Read the checkout address. Look at the address bar on the payment page. A known payment company or retailer is a good sign. A random new domain with the product name in it deserves a second look.
  2. Read the total, not the headline price. Pack size, shipping, tax, and any box that came pre-ticked (insurance, "VIP" club, extra items).
  3. Look for subscription words. "Auto-ship", "membership", "billed every". If you see them and didn't want them, stop.
  4. Find the refund terms before paying. How many days, who pays return shipping, where you send it.
  5. Search the shop name plus "scam" or "reviews". Popular gadgets get copied, and look-alike shops borrow names. Make sure complaints are about the same website you're on.
  6. Pay by credit card. In the US, credit cards come with a legal way to dispute a charge for goods that never arrive. Debit cards and wire transfers give you less.
  7. Keep the order e-mail. It's your proof of the date, the price and the guarantee.

02Red flags

  • A countdown that restarts when you reload the page.
  • "Was" prices several times higher than the price anyone actually pays.
  • No company name, address or phone anywhere on the site.
  • A refund policy that asks you to ship to another country at your own cost.

None of these alone proves anything. Two or three together, and you're better off buying a plain chef knife from a store you know.

··Quick answers

How do I know if a knife website is legit?

Check who runs the checkout (look at the address bar on the payment page), read the full cart total, find the refund terms before paying, and pay by credit card so you can dispute the charge if needed.

Why are there so many shops selling the same knife?

Popular products get copied, and some shops reuse similar names. Reviews you find may be about a different website than the one you're on, so match the web address.
